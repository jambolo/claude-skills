# Pipeline benchmark — 2026-10-22

phase) against the batch-2 definitions: planner 4.0.0, decomposer 4.0.0, supervisor
4.0.0, worker 3.0.0, worker-lite 1.0.0. Raw rows, including the per-model token and
cost breakdown per phase: `2026-10-22-bench-results.tsv`. Costs are Claude Code's
client-side estimates at list price.

## Decisions

- **X3 adopted**: decomposer `effort` high → medium. −21% cost and −14% wall-clock on
  mid with identical outcomes. The supervisor half of X3 was measured on Opus and is not
  carried over; the supervisor keeps `effort: high` under X4.
- **X4 adopted**: supervisor `model` opus → sonnet at `effort: high`. −43% on smoke
  (where Opus was the supervisor alone), −26% on mid. One of the two mid runs retried a
  step once; the baseline never did, so this is outside the noise floor on that metric,
  but the retry was absorbed, every DoD passed, and the cost stayed 26% lower. The
  Sonnet supervisor also resolved the mid fixture's `judgment` step itself.
- **X5 control**: `lite` steps on Sonnet cost 12% more than on Haiku with equal outcomes;
  `worker-lite` stays on `haiku`.
- **Combination confirmed**: the adopted configuration (decomposer opus/medium plus
  supervisor sonnet/high), run twice on mid from the installed definitions, cost 1.77
  and 1.52 USD — mean 1.64 USD, −41% against the 2.81 USD baseline — 19% faster, 3/3
  phases, DoD PASS, no retries or revisions. The savings add, and the single retry
  seen under X4 did not recur.

## Also observed

- Foreground Agent launches issued in one message run concurrently: parallel steps'
  commits landed one second apart with 13–15 s worker durations (smoke run, interactive).
- Opus stages were 88% of baseline mid cost; Sonnet workers 11%; the Haiku driver under
  1%. Prompt caching left fresh input under 200 tokens per phase, so output tokens
  (thinking and files) dominate, which is why effort moves the bill.
- Decomposition was 56% of mid wall-clock (401 of 716 s), workers 26%.
- The supervisor loop costs about 26 s and 17–18 tool uses per two-step phase, of which
  about four calls per step are the launch, verify, decide, merge sequence.
